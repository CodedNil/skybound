# Skybound

A flight game over an alien sky world. A Bevy host (`src/graphics`, `src/game`) drives a rust-gpu shader crate (`src/scene`) compiled to SPIR-V by `build.rs`, which raymarches the whole scene: the aur ocean, clouds, and the creatures and plants of its ecosystem.

## The world

There is no ground. Life drifts or flies above the aur, a glowing, lethal ocean that lights the sky from below and drives the updraft everything depends on. Light is a matter of place, not time: a sunburst lights each pole, a sunset band lies round it, and the equator is lit only by the aur's glow from below. Water is scarce, food drifts in the air, and the taxon descriptions in `src/scene/life` hold each lineage's lore.

Design creatures from this world, not Earth's. No ground means no legs; light and danger come from below, so tetralates have inverted countershading (pale back, dark belly) and downward-looking eyes; scarce water means louvred breathing vents that recover moisture. Eyes are never beads or stalks the wind could tear off: tetralates set lensed eyes in lidded sockets (`Surface::socket`), and the blood-feeders keep lensless cup eyes (`Surface::pit`). Every feature should have a reason that follows from the lore, and related species should still differ in ways their niches explain.

## Building

- Use the dev profile only (`cargo build`, `cargo test`); it is already opt-level 3. Never use `--release`: switching profiles relinks everything and costs many minutes.
- Any edit under `src/scene` re-runs the shader build: two SPIR-V modules built side by side, the game's passes (`scene.spv`) and the previewer's tracer (`preview.spv`, the `preview` feature), each a few minutes.
- Run from `target/debug/skybound`. If the shell predates the flake hook, set `DLSS_SDK=$PWD/vendor/dlss`.
- Shader code can't loop over arrays by value, slice arrays, or call `saturating_sub` or `hypot`; files using `sin`, `sqrt` and so on need `#[cfg(target_arch = "spirv")] use spirv_std::num_traits::Float;`.
- Shader code also can't use `normalize_or`, `normalize_or_zero` or `is_finite`: their check against an infinite length compiles to a constant the GPU's validator rejects, and the world then draws nothing. Divide by a clamped length instead. Or-patterns in a `match` (`5 | 6 =>`) can break the SPIR-V build; use an `if` chain.

## Looking at the result

- The benchmark mode renders offscreen and saves a screenshot: `SKYBOUND_SCREENSHOT=/path/out.png target/debug/skybound --benchmark --scene <scene> --warmup 6 --duration 2`. Scenes are listed in `src/benchmark.rs`; `--altitude`, `--pitch` and `--yaw` give a free camera, and `--live` lets time run. It prints frame times.
- While working on a species, redraw its committed assets in place so the user sees them: `target/debug/examples/species_preview --species <name>` (add `--all` to force it).
- Creature previews are traced on the GPU with the game's own creature tracer (the `preview` compute entry point in `src/scene/preview.rs`, cutaways included), one pipeline per species with the species fixed as a specialisation constant: the tracer with every species' body switched in needs so many registers that it runs about a hundred times slower. `cargo build --example species_preview`, then `target/debug/examples/species_preview --outputs render --out <dir>`. With no `--out` it regenerates the assets committed beside each species file, animated lossy WebP sheets (`_render`, `_anatomy`, `_lifecycle`, `_variation`), redrawing only those older than the code they are drawn from, and, beside every phylum and every taxon where the tree forks, a still 1024 px square WebP sheet named for it (`plantae/plantae.webp`; `--outputs lineup`) of every species below it at true scale, nested together by silhouette.

## Layout

- `src/scene/life`: each species is a short file with a `surface` built from shared primitives in `life/mod.rs` and a `LOOK` (colours and wings). Shading is in `life/surface.rs`.
- Only what all life shares sits at the root: `BIOLOGY` (life history and habitat, `life/biology.rs`), `life/vitals.rs` (a body's state and the rules every body lives by), and `life/organs.rs` (organ placement and wounds). Everything else splits by kingdom: `life/animalia/physiology.rs` and `life/plantae/physiology.rs` each define a `PHYSIOLOGY` that every taxon refines with `..super::PHYSIOLOGY`, and animals alone have a mind (`life/animalia/mind.rs`).
- Every organism has a genome (`life/genome.rs`): genes from zero to one, inherited from both parents with a little change, declared as a `Gene` in the taxon where they arose at a place set by its rank (the root `0..8`, a kingdom `8..20`, a phylum `20..23`, a class `23..26`, an order `26..28`, a family `28..30`, a genus or species `30..32`). Behaviour and looks read genes (`s.temper(GENE)`, `c.gene(GENE)`), never a hash of an id or a roll of chance. Keep what is inherited apart from what is lived: the body's state (`Vitals`: hunger, wounds and where they landed, torn and scarred wings, overgrowth) and the mind's experience (`Experience`: wariness, skill, familiarity) change through a life and are never passed on.
- Bodies move by physics (`life/physics.rs`): weight, buoyancy, drag against the moving air and gliding lift, with wings and gas trim making up the rest as far as the body's state allows; the effort that took drives the wingbeat and what it costs. Join parts with `Surface::blend` where they grow into one another, so both the shape and the material flow across the seam.
- Plants have no behaviour to tell individuals apart, so each grows its own way: a plant's `surface` reads its inherited traits with `plantae::gene`, `pick` and `count` (form, number of parts, size, curl), and individuals of one species should differ drastically, not by a few percent. Keep the parts that hold the organs fixed so the organ test holds for every individual. Plants grow alone and scattered unless their `PHYSIOLOGY.stand` says they grow joined, as kelp forests and bramble chains do.
- A species' `BIOLOGY.size` is how much its grown body is scaled from its drawn design; surfaces and organs stay in the design's frame. Set it so species differ in size as their lore says (the lineup sheets show them at true scale). Young forms are drawn by their lineage, in a `young.rs` beside its `mod.rs` declaring each form's number; `development.rs` only dispatches to them.
- Declare things in the taxon where they arose, never in a list for all life. Organs are named values with the need they meet (`Function`): the pump at the root of the animals, flight muscles separately in the tetralates and the winged blood-feeders, the aur sac in the Nætu lineage, fruit in the fruiting plants, glands in the species that have them. Generic code asks how well a need is met, not for a particular organ. Each species places its organs in `ORGANS`, inside its body (a test checks); they drive its physiology, take the damage from strikes, and show in the anatomy previews.
- Behaviour descends the tree the same way: each animal taxon has an `ETHOLOGY`, taking its parent's with `..super::ETHOLOGY` (or re-exporting it) and overriding how its lineage feels urges, notices things, hunts, courts, sleeps and acts. Intents (`Intent`) and things worth noticing (`Interest`) are declared where they arose, the intent carrying its lineage's way of carrying it out: play and following in the tetralates, calls, the young and grooming in the live-bearers, mobbing in the swarming Manducidae. Put behaviour in a `mod nature` section at the end of that taxon's `mod.rs` or species file, gated out of the shader build. `Perception` holds only what every animal senses; the rest is marks a lineage's `notice` records.
- `src/game/ecosystem/`: the organisms lived out in full near the player, stepped twenty times a second, each organism in parallel: `senses.rs` (what each perceives and wants), `conduct.rs` (strikes, courtship, calls), `feeding.rs`, `breeding.rs`, `movement.rs`, and `drawing.rs` (which are drawn each frame, smoothed between steps). Ask a `Kind` for its habits (`eats`, `takes`, `attaches`, `partners`, `senses`, `voice`) rather than naming species there. `src/game/range.rs`: the whole planet's population as counts per region, limited by each kind's habitat, which comes into being in groups near the player, out to where each kind would still cover a pixel (so giants are met from far off), and folds back as the player leaves.
- `src/scene/render`: clouds (`clouds.rs`, `march.rs`), the aur ocean, and the passes that draw everything.

## Style

- Imports go in one merged block at the top, using `crate::` paths (rustfmt sorts them).
- Comments are full prose sentences that explain the physics or intent, matching the existing code.
